Execution model

Fills assuming zero spread and passive limit orders make mean reversion look much more profitable than it is. ExecutionSimulator simulates each leg independently, against the quote, volume and displayed depth of the bar it fills on.

Market orders

\[p_{\text{fill}} = m \pm \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\text{spread} + m\,\eta\,\sigma_{\text{bar}}\sqrt{q / V}\right)\]

σ_bar is an EWMA of each asset's bar returns, V is the bar volume and η is impact_coef.

Liquidity checks

Each bar's fill is capped at min(max_participation · V, depth_levels · depth). Anything left over carries to the next bar as a partial fill. If a bar has no volume, the order waits.

Passive orders and legging risk

With style = :passive, both legs rest as limit orders at the touch quoted on the decision bar. A limit order fills only on a strict trade-through: for a buy, the next bid has to be below the limit price. That builds in adverse selection, because passive fills happen when the price has moved against you.

When one leg fills and its sibling hasn't, the sibling becomes a market order and crosses the spread latency bars later. Its fill is flagged as legged. A limit order that hasn't filled within limit_wait bars crosses as well.

In the demo, passive execution pays about 42 bps of effective spread against about 2 bps for market orders, and its Sharpe ratio turns negative.

Multi-leg tickets

submit_ticket! sends any number of legs as one ticket (baskets use 3–5). Legging generalises directly: the first passive fill on any leg turns every other resting leg of the ticket into a market order.

Re-pegging

With limit_wait > 1, a resting order normally keeps its decision-bar price. Setting reprice = true re-pegs it to each new touch. Both stay off by default (limit_wait = 1, reprice = false). On daily synthetic bars, resting longer, with or without re-pegging, produced more legged fills (74 → 160 per run) and a lower Sharpe ratio (−0.98 → −1.19) than crossing after one bar.

Halts and missing data

panel.tradable is false on bars that were missing in the source data, had zero volume, or were marked halted. On those bars:

  • no order fills; resting and in-flight orders simply wait;
  • pairs don't update their filters or ring buffers, because a stale quote carries no new information;
  • no new positions open, although exits keep their signal and fill once trading resumes.

Corporate actions

A CorporateAction records a split ratio and/or a cash dividend on its ex-date. Panel prices are raw, so they jump on the ex-date. Before that bar's fills, apply_corporate_actions!:

  1. credits the dividend to long positions and debits it from short positions;
  2. multiplies share positions by the split ratio;
  3. rescales resting orders: quantity × split, and limit and reference prices ÷ split.

Signals read logmid, which is adjusted forward in time only. That keeps it continuous through the event without ever depending on actions that haven't happened yet. A test injects a 2-for-1 split into the raw data and checks that the equity curve doesn't change (beyond share rounding).

Margin and forced liquidation

After each bar's fills, the backtester compares equity with risk.maintenance_margin × gross exposure. If equity falls below that, or to zero or less, it records a margin call, cancels every resting order and closes every position at market. New entries are then blocked for risk.liquidation_cooldown bars, or permanently if equity is gone. The dashboard lists every margin call.

Allocations are capped at risk.max_gross when they're decided. Rebalance hysteresis (min_trade_frac) skips small adjustments, unless skipping them would leave the book above the cap. In that case, every open pair is brought to its jointly capped target.

Financing

accrue_financing! charges, for every bar:

  • a borrow fee on the market value of each short position (borrow_rate, with optional per-asset borrow_rates);
  • interest on cash, excluding short-sale proceeds, which are held as collateral. A positive balance earns cash_rate and a negative one pays margin_rate.

Cost attribution

Every fill is logged in res.fills with its spread cost relative to the mid, its impact cost, its commission, and whether it was passive or legged. cost_breakdown adds these up in currency units and in basis points of traded notional.
