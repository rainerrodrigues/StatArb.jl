Execution model

Fills assuming zero spread and passive limit orders make mean reversion look much more profitable than it is. ExecutionSimulator simulates each leg independently, against the quote, volume and displayed depth of the bar it fills on.

Market orders

\[p_{\text{fill}} = m \pm \left(\tfrac{1}{2}\text{spread} + m\,\eta\,\sigma_{\text{bar}}\sqrt{q / V}\right)\]

σ_bar is an EWMA of each asset's bar returns, V is the bar volume and η is impact_coef.

Liquidity checks

Each bar's fill is capped at min(max_participation · V, depth_levels · depth). Anything left over carries to the next bar as a partial fill. If a bar has no volume, the order waits.

Passive orders and legging risk

With style = :passive, both legs rest as limit orders at the touch quoted on the decision bar. A limit order fills only on a strict trade-through: for a buy, the next bid has to be below the limit price. That builds in adverse selection, because passive fills happen when the price has moved against you.

When one leg fills and its sibling hasn't, the sibling becomes a market order and crosses the spread latency bars later. Its fill is flagged as legged. A limit order that hasn't filled within limit_wait bars crosses as well.

In the demo, passive execution pays about 46 bps of effective spread against about 2 bps for market orders, and its Sharpe ratio turns negative.

Financing

accrue_financing! charges, for every bar:

  • a borrow fee on the market value of each short position (borrow_rate, with optional per-asset borrow_rates);
  • interest on cash, excluding short-sale proceeds, which are held as collateral. A positive balance earns cash_rate and a negative one pays margin_rate.

Cost attribution

Every fill is logged in res.fills with its spread cost relative to the mid, its impact cost, its commission, and whether it was passive or legged. cost_breakdown adds these up in currency units and in basis points of traded notional.
