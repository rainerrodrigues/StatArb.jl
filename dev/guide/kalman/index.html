Kalman hedge ratios

The state $\theta_t = [\alpha_t, \beta_t]'$ follows a random walk and is observed through the log-price regression:

\[y_t = F_t \theta_t + \epsilon_t,\quad F_t = [1, x_t],\quad \epsilon_t \sim \mathcal N(0, R); \qquad \theta_t = \theta_{t-1} + \omega_t,\quad \omega_t \sim \mathcal N(0, Q)\]

Allocation-free updates

KalmanHedge stores θ as an SVector{2} and P and Q as SMatrix{2,2} fields of a mutable struct. The observation is scalar, so the innovation covariance S_t is a scalar and the gain needs a division, not a matrix inversion. The covariance update uses the Joseph form for numerical stability. kalman_update! takes about 30 ns and makes 0 allocations.

Uncertainty-aware signals

The trading Z-score is the standardised innovation

\[z_t = \frac{y_t - F_t \theta_{t-1}}{\sqrt{S_t}}, \qquad S_t = F_t P_{t|t-1} F_t' + R\]

It is not a residual divided by a rolling standard deviation: when the filter becomes less sure of the hedge ratio, S_t grows and the same price move produces a smaller signal. On top of that, size_multiplier scales position size by min(1, P_ref / P_ββ), where P_ref is the steady-state variance at the end of calibration.

Calibration

calibrate_kalman works in three steps on the formation window:

  1. OLS gives the initial θ and the residual variance σ², which sets R = r_scale · σ².
  2. The OLS covariance, scaled up by the sample size n, gives a diffuse prior.
  3. The filter is run through the formation window, so that P reaches steady state before any live trading.

KalmanConfig sets Q in a unit-free form of Chan's δ:

\[Q = q_{\text{scale}} \cdot \frac{\delta}{1-\delta} \cdot \sigma^2 \cdot \mathrm{diag}(1, 1/\bar x^2)\]

The textbook δ/(1−δ)·I depends on price units. On log prices it lets the intercept absorb the spread itself, and the filter then produces almost no trading signals.

Reading the telemetry

The dashboard reports the lag-1 autocorrelation of the innovations. A correctly specified filter produces white innovations; the test suite checks this with known Q and R. A high value means the spread has dynamics the random-walk-coefficient model doesn't capture, for example Ornstein-Uhlenbeck mean reversion.
